Democrats ask Pentagon about Iran school strike and role of AI

In a letter to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, more than 120 Democratic lawmakers ask whether the Pentagon will investigate the strike as a possible war crime.

President Donald Trump speaks to reporters aboard Air Force One. Evan Vucci / AP file

March 12, 2026, 8:14 AM GMT+2

By Courtney Kube and Carol E. Lee

WASHINGTON — More than 120 Democratic lawmakers are demanding answers from Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth about a recent U.S. airstrike on a school in Iran, including whether artificial intelligence was used to identify the target.

In a letter sent to the Pentagon on Thursday and obtained first by NBC News, the lawmakers expressed “grave concern” over the strike, which local officials and human rights groups say resulted in significant civilian casualties.

“We write to request immediate clarification regarding the intelligence processes and targeting directives that led to the authorization of this strike,” the lawmakers, led by Rep. Jason Crow, D-Colo., and Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., wrote in the letter.

The Biden administration has previously defended the use of advanced algorithms in conflict zones, arguing they reduce collateral damage. However, the Democrats’ letter questions the reliability of those systems and asks whether human oversight was bypassed.

The Pentagon did not immediately respond to a request for comment on the letter.
